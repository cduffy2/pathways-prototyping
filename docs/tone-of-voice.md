# Tone of voice

How to write anything users see: headings, labels, buttons, descriptions, and caveats.

Sources: the [Pathways Communications Style Guide 2025](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/194_EHCAyguQMwR3CTTk7LtdxkAuJPDTM/edit) (lead source; Chicago Manual of Style for anything it doesn't cover), Zeroheight [Preferred Terms](https://pathways.zeroheight.com/styleguide/s/102143/p/74bff2), and copy on the live site and in the Figma feature files.

## Voice

Clear, practical, and respectful. Users are policymakers, donors, analysts, and implementers with a wide range of data literacy.

- **Plain language.** Say what a number means: "Woman did not receive any antenatal care visit for her most recent birth", not "ANC0 = 1".
- **Practical, not promotional.** Say what the user can do. No hype ("transform", "revolutionise").
- **Respectful.** Segments describe circumstances, not character. Use "vulnerability" with care, so you don't label or "other" people: say what they are vulnerable *to* ("their specific vulnerabilities to poor health").
- **Woman-centred.** The data is about women and their children. Write from their circumstances.

## Mechanics

- **UK English.** Spell: analyse, behaviour, programme (not "program", unless software), healthcare, dataset, policymaker, decision-making, sociocultural, modelling, co-design, under-5. Use *-ise* verbs: visualise, prioritise.
- **Sentence case** for UI text: headings, buttons, tabs, nav and labels. Never ALL CAPS. (The style guide uses title case for headings in documents and events; the platform UI doesn't.)
- **No full stop** after headings or button labels. Use them in descriptions that are full sentences.
- **Serial comma:** "social, economic, and environmental factors".
- **En dash for ranges** with no spaces: "15–49", "50%–60%", "10–10:30 am BST".
- **Numbers:** spell out one to nine, numerals from 10. Always numerals for percentages ("45%", no space), ages, dates and statistics. Commas from 1,000.
- **Dates:** 1 October 2025, or Monday, 1 October 2025. **Times:** 12-hour with time zone, "2–4:15 pm BST".
- **Data** is singular: "the data shows".
- **Lists:** capitalise the first word, no end punctuation unless items are full sentences. Numbered lists only for steps.
- **Segment codes:** R3.1.

## Terminology

From the style guide, where terms were tested with Pathways users. Use them exactly, including capitals, in UI copy, page names and placeholder content. Terms marked † come from the live platform rather than the guide. Fuller definitions are in the Pathways Glossary (linked from the style guide).

### The platform

| Use | Meaning | Don't use |
|---|---|---|
| **digital platform** | Everything users reach: data, tools and resources | product, application, resource, Segment Explorer |
| **tools** | The parts of the platform users work in (Data browser, Comparison tool, Prevalence map) | — |
| **website** | Only the landing pages | — |
| **Pathways** or **Pathways initiative** (lowercase i) | The whole endeavour: approach, tools and technical support | Pathways project |
| **project** | One funded piece of work that uses a specific segmentation, e.g. "Pathways projects around the world" | initiative (for a single project) |
| **Pathways Community** (capital C) | People and organisations who use, contribute to and support Pathways | Pathways community |

### The approach and data

| Use | Meaning | Don't use |
|---|---|---|
| **Pathways approach** | How Pathways combines qualitative and quantitative data to group households into segments | methodology, framework |
| **segmentation** or **population segmentation** | Grouping households by social, economic, cultural and environmental conditions, not demographics or behaviours | — |
| **segment** or **population segment** | One group from a segmentation, e.g. "women in this segment" | — |
| **segment profile** † | A segment's data and insights in one place | — |
| **domains** | The six groups of factors: the woman and her past experiences; household relationships; household economics and living conditions; social support; health mental models; human and natural systems. Use these names exactly. | — |
| **vulnerability factors** † | Conditions that put women at greater risk of poor health | — |
| **health outcomes and behaviours** | What the data measures about health | — |
| **social determinants of health** | Non-medical factors that shape daily life | — |
| **Pathways Survey** (capital S) | The household survey of women of reproductive age | Pathways survey |
| **Pathways typing tools** (lowercase t) | Short question sets that place an individual into a segment | Pathways Typing Tools |
| **findings** or **insights** | What the analysis shows | results, information |
| **quantitative data**, **qualitative data** | — | — |
| **woman-centred** (exactly this form) | e.g. "woman-centred data" | women-centred, woman-centered |

### Vulnerability

"Vulnerability" means being at greater risk of poor health because of social, cultural, behavioural, economic and environmental factors. Use it with care:

- Describe what people are vulnerable *to*: "their specific vulnerabilities to poor health".
- Levels describe segments, not people: "Most vulnerable segment", never "the most vulnerable women".
- Don't use "vulnerability lens".

### Words that work, and words to avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| improve, strengthen | transform, accelerate, enhance, innovate |
| data-driven decision-making, health equity, human-centred design | design-driven, precision global health, vulnerability lens |

### What users do with Pathways

When copy needs to describe the value of a tool, use the four use cases: **Forecast** expected impact, **Resource** more effectively, **Design** for specific segments, **Evaluate** impact against a baseline. Users include ministries of health, donors, researchers and implementing partners.

## UI labels

| Element | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Page title | Short noun phrase | "Data browser", "Comparison tool" |
| Page intro | One sentence on what you can do | "Explore how population segments experience individual health outcomes, behaviours, and vulnerability factors." |
| Button, menu item | Verb + object | "Change data point", "Share this view" |
| Link | Verb, with an arrow | "Read more →", "View all →" |
| Toggle | Noun | "Standard error" |
| Closing a sheet | Plain instruction | "Close this window" |

## Data labels

- **Data point names** match live exactly, everywhere (chart, list, export).
- **Description:** one plain sentence starting with who and what: "The percentage of women who received a comprehensive postnatal health assessment…"
- **Denominator:** who was asked, shown only when it differs from the segmentation's default sample: "Women aged 15–49 with at least one birth in the five years preceding the survey".
- **Benchmarks:** "Sample average" (bar), "Average" (stacked bar), "Sample median" (box plot).
- **Missing data:** "N/A". Never "0" or "No data available".

## Caveats

State the limitation, then what it means for the user, in one or two sentences.

| Situation | Copy |
|---|---|
| Survey not representative below national level | "This segmentation is based on the Pathways Survey, which is not designed to yield exact state-level results. Interpret this data with caution." Dismissible, with a link to source data details. |
| Segment excluded | "One urban segment is not included here (1% of the population) due to uncertainty in estimates owing to its very small size." |
| Tool not built for small screens | "Limited mobile experience" / "This page works best on larger screens." |
| Prototype data | Never imply it's real. Label it "Illustrative data" if it could be mistaken for findings. |
